1. Motivation
Empathy plays a foundational role in interactive AI systems, especially in human–robot interaction
(HRI), where empathic behavior influences trust, cooperation, and long-term acceptance [3, 1]. Cru-
cially, empathy in HRI is highly contextual: its expression—and users’ perception of it—varies widely
across tasks, roles, and interaction settings. A collaborative robot in an assembly task may express
empathy through adaptive task coordination (e.g., slowing down when a human hesitates), whereas a
counseling-oriented conversational agent demonstrates empathy through reflective dialogue. A home
service robot may instead convey empathy by anticipating user needs or adjusting behavior in response
to frustration. Although all represent “empathy,” their forms differ sharply across platforms and use
cases.
This variability makes measurement difficult. Existing instruments such as RoPE [4] and PETS
[12] provide useful baselines but are either too specialized or too general to capture scenario-dependent
differences. As a result, researchers lack tools that reflect how empathy should appear within a specific
interaction context.
Developing scenario-specific empathy scales traditionally requires the full nine-step psychometric
framework [2], including construct definition, item generation, expert review, pilot testing, and factor
analysis. While rigorous, this process is slow, costly, and impractical to repeat for the diverse and rapidly
expanding range of HRI scenarios.
Advances in large language models (LLMs) offer a potential path toward scalable solutions. LLMs
can generate psychometric items [7, 8] and perform structured evaluation tasks such as UX analysis
[6]. Multi-agent LLM systems such as ChatDev [11] and MetaGPT [5] further show that coordinated,
role-specialized agents can emulate complex expert workflows. These developments motivate exploring
whether similar architectures can support rapid creation of scenario-sensitive empathy scales for HRI.
This project aims to develop a lightweight, non-iterative multi-agent LLM prototype that generates
scenario-specific empathy items and conducts preliminary evaluation using simulated “participants.”
Comprehensive validation with human users is reserved for future work.

3. Problem Formulation & Expected Outcomes
Objective: Develop a multi-agent LLM system to generate scenario-sensitive empathy items and con-
duct initial LLM-based evaluation.
Research questions: (1) Can multi-agent LLMs generate coherent, scenario-specific empathy items?
(2) Can LLM evaluation agents provide consistent preliminary judgments? (3) Do generated items ap-
pear more contextually appropriate than baseline scales such as PETS?
System components: Construct Definition Agents defines scenario-relevant empathy dimensions.
Item Generation Agents produce diverse candidate items (6–12 per scenario). Content Assessment
Agents refines wording and removes redundancy.Evaluation Agents simulate participant judgments of
clarity, relevance, and scenario sensitivity.
The system will produce two to three scenario-specific empathy scales (e.g., robot teammate, home
robot, counseling agent). No factor analysis or human user evaluation is included in this phase.
4. Planned Methods
The prototype will be implemented in Python using LLM APIs. Given a scenario description, the Con-
struct Definition Agent extracts relevant constructs. Multiple Item Generation Agents independently
generate candidate items to increase diversity. A Content Assessment Agent performs wording improve-
ments and content consolidation. Evaluation Agents simulate human participants by providing Likert
ratings and short rationales for each item. All evaluations are LLM-based and treated as feasibility
signals rather than formal psychometric evidence.
No human data will be collected. Ethical considerations are limited, but any future real-user studies
would require formal review.
5. Evaluation Plan
The system will be evaluated through LLM-based participant simulation. Evaluation agents will provide
item-level clarity ratings, relevance ratings, scenario-sensitivity judgments, and concise justifications.
Both SensEmoScale-generated items and baseline scales (PETS [12] and RoPE [4]) will be evaluated
under identical procedures.
Metrics include mean clarity, contextual relevance, differences across scenarios, and stability across
repeated simulation runs. Ablations will compare single-agent versus multi-agent generation, with ver-
sus without content refinement, and scenario-specific versus generic prompting. The evaluation is pre-
liminary and does not constitute psychometric validation; full statistical analysis and user studies remain
future work.
