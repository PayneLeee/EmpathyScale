# Empathy Scale

## Purpose
This scale measures the human operator’s perceived empathy of a musculoskeletal robot collaborator during a shared assembly task. Perceived empathy is defined as the robot’s ability to sense, interpret, and appropriately respond to the human’s spatial presence, intentions, and comfort in real‑time, fostering a considerate and fluid collaboration. Items capture the key empathy forms: timely intent indication, yielding behaviour, space management, and avoidance of uncomfortable contact.

## Items
Participants rate each statement on a 5‑point Likert scale:  
1 – Strongly Disagree  
2 – Disagree  
3 – Neutral  
4 – Agree  
5 – Strongly Agree  

1. The robot clearly indicated when it was about to reach for an item (e.g., by saying “我来拿这个” or by pausing its arm).  
2. When I reached for a tool, the robot often yielded or gave way without me having to ask.  
3. The robot left enough space for me to work comfortably at the shared tool rack.  
4. The robot adjusted its arm trajectory to avoid getting too close to my body.  
5. I felt that the robot was aware of where I was reaching and tried not to block me.  
6. The robot sometimes bumped into me or made contact without any attempt to avoid it. (R)  
7. The robot’s verbal cues helped me anticipate its next move and coordinate smoothly.  
8. The robot seemed to care about my personal space and proximity during the task.  
9. When we both reached for the same part, the robot quickly paused or retracted to let me go first.  
10. Overall, the robot’s behaviour made me feel that it was considerate of my actions and comfort.

(R) denotes a reverse‑scored item.
