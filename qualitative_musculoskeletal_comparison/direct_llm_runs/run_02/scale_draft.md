# Empathy Scale

## Purpose
This scale measures the extent to which a human operator perceives empathetic behavior from a musculoskeletal robot during shared-space assembly collaboration. Empathy is operationalized as the robot’s ability to anticipate the human’s actions, communicate intent, yield space, and avoid or mitigate collisions in a manner that feels considerate and human-like.

## Items
Respondents rate each statement on a 5-point Likert scale:  
1 = Strongly Disagree, 2 = Disagree, 3 = Neutral, 4 = Agree, 5 = Strongly Agree.

1. The robot gave clear signals (verbal or visual) before reaching into the shared tool rack so I could anticipate its movement.
2. When we both reached for the same area, the robot paused or withdrew to let me take what I needed first.
3. The robot maintained a comfortable distance from my body while we worked side by side.
4. If the robot’s arm came close to me, it slowed down or changed its path to avoid accidental contact.
5. The robot seemed to notice when I needed more space at the rack and adjusted its reach accordingly.
6. After any minor collision or brush, the robot paused or repositioned in a way that felt apologetic or careful.
7. The robot’s timing felt cooperative – it waited for me to finish my reach before moving into the same space.
8. The robot’s arm trajectories felt similar to how a human collaborator would move – considerate rather than mechanical.
9. When the robot spoke (e.g., “我来拿这个” or “你先拿”), it helped me understand its intentions and reduced uncertainty.
10. Overall, the robot acted as if it understood my physical presence and tried to avoid interfering with my movements.
