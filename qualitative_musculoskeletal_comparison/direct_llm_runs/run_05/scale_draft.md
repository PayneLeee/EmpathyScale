# Empathy Scale

## Purpose
This Likert scale measures the human collaborator's perception of empathy exhibited by a musculoskeletal robot during side-by-side assembly tasks. Perceived empathy is defined as the robot's ability to anticipate the human's actions, communicate its own intentions, respect the human's spatial and temporal needs, and adjust its movements to maintain smooth, safe, and considerate collaboration. The scale targets four key empathy forms: timely indication of intent, yielding or giving way, leaving space at shared resources, and consideration of the human's spatial comfort and proximity.

## Items
Respondents indicate their level of agreement with each statement using a 7-point Likert scale:  
1 = Strongly Disagree, 2 = Disagree, 3 = Somewhat Disagree, 4 = Neutral, 5 = Somewhat Agree, 6 = Agree, 7 = Strongly Agree.

1. The robot gave clear advance signals (e.g., verbal cues like “我来拿这个” or visible pauses) before reaching for a tool, so I could anticipate its move.

2. When I reached for an item, the robot yielded by pausing, retracting, or changing its path to avoid interfering with my hand.

3. The robot maintained enough space between its arm and my body during the task, making me feel physically comfortable.

4. The robot often reached for the same shelf slot at the same time I did, causing blocking or collision. (reverse-coded)

5. When our trajectories crossed, the robot did not adjust its path or slow down, and made contact without any explanation or pause. (reverse-coded)

6. The robot seemed to notice when I needed extra room at the shared tool rack and intentionally left space for me.

7. The robot's movements (arm pauses, path changes, retractions) clearly indicated its next action, helping me coordinate my own reach.

8. After a close encounter or minor contact, the robot responded by pausing, moving away, or saying something like “你先拿” to acknowledge the situation.

9. I felt the robot was considerate of my personal space and proximity, similar to how a human coworker would behave.

10. The robot frequently blocked my reach or brushed against me without any attempt to avoid it. (reverse-coded)

11. The robot’s verbal and visual signals were timed well enough that I never felt surprised by its movements.

12. The robot treated the shared workspace as a mutual zone, adjusting its own behavior to fit my actions rather than assuming I would always move out of its way.
