# Empathy Scale (Draft)

## Purpose and Context
This scale is designed to measure perceived empathy in a home-assistant robot that supports users with daily tasks. It assesses the robot's ability to understand and respond to user emotions, particularly in situations involving frustration and the need for reassurance.

## Structure
- **Dimensions/Subscales**:
  - Cognitive Empathy: Understanding user emotions and perspectives.
  - Affective Empathy: Responding to user emotions with sensitivity and care.
  - Behavioral Empathy: Demonstrating appropriate actions in response to emotional cues.

- **Response Format**: 5-point Likert scale (1 = Strongly Disagree, 5 = Strongly Agree).
- **Administration Notes**: Users rate the robot's perceived empathy based on their interactions. Assessments can be made following task completion or during emotional exchanges.

## Items by Dimension
### Dimension A: Cognitive Empathy
- Item 1: The robot detects changes in my speech patterns indicating frustration. 
- Item 2: The robot recognizes my body language when I'm upset.
- Item 3: The robot understands my needs without me having to explain. 

### Dimension B: Affective Empathy
- Item 1: The robot uses calming gestures when I feel anxious.
- Item 2: The robot reassures me effectively through speech.
- Item 3: The robot provides comforting words when I am stressed.
- Item 4: The robot shows sensitivity to my emotional state.

### Dimension C: Behavioral Empathy
- Item 1: The robot senses when I could use a hand.
- Item 2: The robot anticipates my needs without me asking.
- Item 3: The robot recognizes when I need assistance and acts.

## Scoring
The scale uses a 5-point Likert response format. Higher scores reflect greater perceived empathy, with subscale totals calculated for specific dimensions. All items are positively scored, but reverse-scored items will be included for validation (e.g., “The robot does not react to my frustration.”).

## Design Rationale

### Theoretical Framework and Dimension Selection
The empathy scale is based on established frameworks of cognitive, affective, and behavioral empathy, which reflect the multi-faceted nature of empathy (Charrier et al., 2019; Putta et al., 2022). Cognitive empathy allows the robot to understand the user's emotional state (Rizvi et al., 2018), while affective empathy emphasizes the emotional resonance and connection (Schmidmaier et al., 2024). Behavioral empathy encompasses actions taken by the robot in response to detected emotions. Each dimension is critical for successful human-robot collaboration in a domestic environment, especially when focusing on user comfort and perceived care.

### Item Design and Phrasing Rationale
1. **Cognitive Empathy**:
   - Items assess the robot’s ability to recognize verbal and non-verbal cues (Frustration Detection). The adaptation reflects user expectations for the robot to respond to emotional indicators, supported by findings from Schmidmaier et al. (2024) and Charrier et al. (2019).

2. **Affective Empathy**:
   - Items emphasize emotional reactions and reassurances, which are vital for enhancing emotional bonds (Schmidmaier et al., 2024). Calming gestures and comforting speech are crucial for a home assistant robot, ensuring that the robot’s responses resonate with user emotions.

3. **Behavioral Empathy**:
   - Items focus on proactive assistance capabilities expected of the robot in a domestic routine (Putta et al., 2022). These elements are critical as they enhance user trust and reliance on the robot when it anticipates and responds to needs.

### Response Format Justification
A 5-point Likert scale was selected as it provides a balance between granularity of responses and ease of interpretation, aligning with best practices in empathy measurement (Boateng et al., 2018). The descriptive anchors ensure clarity, while the scale allows users to express varying levels of perceived empathy.

### Adaptation Rationale
- **Item Modifications**: 
   - The initial list was tailored to focus on home interactions, emphasizing usable language and scenarios typical of a home assistant robot. Items were added or modified based on findings from Putta et al. (2022) and Schmidmaier et al. (2024), which highlighted specific emotional responses relevant in everyday settings.
   - For instance, "The robot understands my needs without me explaining" supports cognitive evaluations of empathy that align with user interactions within domestic tasks (Rizvi et al., 2018).

## Brief Rationales for Modifications
- Shifted focus towards proactive and emotional responses following feedback from users emphasizing the need for reassurance capabilities.
- Added dimension-specific items grounded in literature to reflect contextual and operational realities in home environments, ensuring robust measurement of user experiences.

## Notes for Validation
- It is recommended to conduct pilot testing with target users to evaluate scale clarity and relevance.
- Assess internal consistency and test-retest reliability for establishing scale stability over time.
