# Empathy Scale (Draft)

## Purpose and Context
- This scale is designed to assess the perceived empathy of a text-based counseling chatbot during supportive dialogue interactions. It aims to measure emotional attunement between users and the chatbot, focusing on reflective listening and emotional validation.

## Structure
- **Dimensions/Subscales**:
  - Reflective Listening: The extent to which the chatbot mirrors the user's feelings and thoughts.
  - Emotional Validation: The chatbot's ability to acknowledge and accept the user's emotions.
  - Supportive Tone: The quality of the chatbot's communication style that promotes a positive user experience.
  - Empathetic Inquiry: The chatbot's encouragement of deeper exploration of the user's emotions through questioning.

- **Response Format**: 5-point Likert scale (1 = Strongly Disagree, 5 = Strongly Agree).
- **Administration Notes**: Users rate their experience with the chatbot following interactions, providing insight into the chatbot's empathy effectiveness.

## Items by Dimension
### Dimension A: Reflective Listening
- Item 1: The chatbot accurately reflects my feelings.
- Item 2: I feel understood when the chatbot paraphrases my thoughts.
- Item 3: The chatbot summarizes my feelings and concerns effectively.
  
### Dimension B: Emotional Validation
- Item 1: I feel accepted when the chatbot validates my feelings.
- Item 2: The chatbot acknowledges my emotions without judgment.
- Item 3: I feel heard when the chatbot affirms my experiences.

### Dimension C: Supportive Tone
- Item 1: I feel encouraged by the chatbot's supportive responses.
- Item 2: The chatbot's tone conveys care and understanding.
- Item 3: The chatbot creates a safe space for my thoughts.

### Dimension D: Empathetic Inquiry
- Item 1: I appreciate the open-ended questions from the chatbot.
- Item 2: The chatbot invites me to explore my feelings deeply.
- Item 3: The chatbot asks questions that encourage me to share more.

## Scoring
- Total scores for each dimension are obtained by summing the relevant item ratings. Higher scores indicate greater perceived empathy. Reverse-coded items are not included, ensuring simplicity in interpretation.

## Design Rationale

### Theoretical Framework and Dimension Selection
The scale is grounded in established empathy literature, particularly focused on the three primary constructs of empathy: cognitive, affective, and behavioral (Davis, 1983; Eisenberg & Strayer, 1987). Each dimension reflects the nuanced elements of empathy relevant to human-robot collaboration as articulated by Charrier et al. (2019), who emphasize the importance of reflective listening and validation in empathetic interactions with robots. Emotional validation and reflective listening are crucial for fostering connection in a remote, text-based counseling context (Baumeister & Leary, 1995). Therefore, the inclusion of supportive tone and empathetic inquiry strengthens the overall impact of the chatbot as a helpful companion.

### Item Design and Phrasing Rationale
- Reflective Listening: Each item taps into the cognitive dimension of empathy, exploring how well the chatbot can capture and reflect the user's emotional state. Items employ straightforward, user-focused language to resonate with the user's experience.
- Emotional Validation: These items are crafted to measure affective responses, ensuring they convey acceptance and acknowledgment of the user's feelings. The phrasing aligns with practices suggested by Putta et al. (2022), underscoring the importance of non-judgmental validation in digital interactions.
- Supportive Tone: This dimension investigates how the chatbot's communication style fosters a welcoming environment. The phrasing reflects user expectations of warmth and care, essential in therapeutic contexts (Schmidmaier et al., 2024).
- Empathetic Inquiry: These items are designed to assess behavioral empathy, focusing on how the chatbot's questioning strategies support deeper emotional expression. Open-ended frames encourage user engagement, a technique evidenced to enhance rapport in therapy settings (Wampold & Imel, 2015).

### Response Format Justification
A 5-point Likert scale was chosen to balance sensitivity and usability, facilitating nuanced feedback without overwhelming users (Boateng et al., 2018). This format aligns well with existing empathy measurement tools and has been shown to effectively capture subjective experiences in similar contexts (Charrier et al., 2019).

### Adaptation Rationale
- The scale utilizes core items from existing validated measures (Charrier et al., 2019; Putta et al., 2022) but has been tailored for the specific context of a text-based counseling bot. For example, items were simplified to ensure clarity and direct relevance to the remote interaction setting. 
- Adjustments reflect findings from Schmidmaier et al. (2024), who emphasized the need for concise and clear evaluations of technology-mediated empathy experiences. Each item modification reflects the insight that users engage more effectively with straightforward, context-relevant prompts, preserving conceptual validity while enhancing contextual fit.

## Brief Rationales for Modifications
- Items were condensed to improve clarity without losing essential empathetic elements (Schmidmaier et al., 2024).
- Phrasing was adapted to suit the text-based interaction format recorded in user interviews, ensuring resonance with user experiences (Putta et al., 2022).
- Items reflecting emotional validation were emphasized based on user feedback indicating a strong need for acceptance and affirmation in chatbot interactions (Charrier et al., 2019).

## Notes for Validation
- Pilot testing with a representative user sample to assess scale clarity and reliability.
- Analyzing item inter-correlations can enhance understanding of subscale validity.
- Longitudinal studies could assess stability of empathy perceptions over multiple interactions.
