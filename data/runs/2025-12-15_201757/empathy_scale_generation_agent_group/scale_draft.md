# Empathy Scale (Draft)

## Purpose and Context
This empathy scale is designed to assess the perceived empathy of collaborative robots (cobots) in a factory assembly setting, specifically when they interact with human teammates. It aims to evaluate safety awareness, adaptive pacing, and empathy-related behaviors such as mirroring and proactive assistance, thereby optimizing human-robot collaboration.

## Structure
- **Dimensions/Subscales**:
  - **Safety Awareness**: Measures the robot's actions and alerts regarding potential dangers in the work environment.
  - **Adaptive Pacing**: Evaluates how the robot adjusts its speed and movements according to the human's pace.
  - **Hesitation Mirroring**: Assesses the robot's capacity to mirror human hesitation and uncertainty during collaborative tasks.
  - **Proactive Assistance**: Looks at the robot's ability to anticipate and provide help before human intervention is requested.

- **Response Format**: 
  - 5-point Likert scale (1 = Strongly Disagree; 5 = Strongly Agree).

- **Administration Notes**: 
  - This scale should be filled out by human operatives after interactions with the robot. It is recommended to use this scale immediately following task completion to ensure reflections are based on fresh experiences.

## Items by Dimension

### Dimension A: Safety Awareness
- Item 1: The robot visually displays alerts for nearby dangers.
- Item 2: The robot adjusts its actions to avoid risks to the human.
- Item 3: The robot stops its actions when detecting a potential hazard.
- Item 4: The robot alerts the human to unsafe conditions promptly.

### Dimension B: Adaptive Pacing
- Item 1: The robot adjusts its speed based on my movements.
- Item 2: The robot speeds up when the human is ready for faster work.
- Item 3: The robot slows down when the human is working at a slower pace.
- Item 4: The robot matches its actions to my pace.

### Dimension C: Hesitation Mirroring
- Item 1: The robot adjusts its actions when it senses human hesitation.
- Item 2: The robot reflects similar gestures when the human shows uncertainty.
- Item 3: The robot pauses when the human hesitates during tasks.
- Item 4: The robot reflects my timing in assembly actions.

### Dimension D: Proactive Assistance
- Item 1: The robot suggests the next step before the human asks.
- Item 2: The robot initiates support during challenging tasks.
- Item 3: The robot offers help when it detects the human is overwhelmed.
- Item 4: The robot anticipates human needs based on task progress.

## Scoring
- Each item is scored on a 5-point Likert scale. 
- Higher scores indicate greater perceived empathy.
- Reverse-coded items (if applicable) should be identified during analysis.
- Subscale totals can be calculated for each dimension, with an overall score reflecting total empathy.

## Design Rationale

### Theoretical Framework and Dimension Selection
The empathy scale is based on a multidimensional framework that includes cognitive, affective, and behavioral empathy (Charrier et al., 2019). Cognitive empathy allows understanding of human needs, while affective empathy relates to emotional resonance, and behavioral empathy focuses on actions taken in response to human emotions (Charrier et al., 2019; Putta et al., 2022). Each dimension selected maps directly to the core functions robots carry out in human-robot collaboration in factory settings, ensuring relevant evaluation of empathy.

### Item Design and Phrasing Rationale
Each item was developed based on established indicators of empathetic behaviors in robots. The items reflect both operational safety and interpersonal support, crucial for the assembly context:
- **Safety Awareness** items draw from existing literature emphasizing the importance of safety signals and adjustments (Putta et al., 2022).
- **Adaptive Pacing** reflects the need for robots to synchronize their actions with human workers, a finding supported by empirical observations of collaborative robot assistance (Schmidmaier et al., 2024).
- **Hesitation Mirroring** items were designed based on the concept of mirroring behaviors in both human and robot interactions that facilitate smoother collaboration (Charrier et al., 2019; Schmidmaier et al., 2024).
- **Proactive Assistance** items assess anticipatory behavior which is critical in time-sensitive factory settings (Putta et al., 2022), enhancing collaborative efficiency.

### Response Format Justification
A 5-point Likert scale was chosen for its balance between granularity and ease of use, suitable for busy factory environments (Schmidmaier et al., 2024). Likert scales have been effective in measuring attitudinal constructs (Boateng et al., 2018). Anchors were labeled to clearly express positive and negative sentiments, aligning with best practices in psychometrics (Boateng et al., 2018).

### Adaptation Rationale
1. **Item Revision Based on Context**: The phrasing of items was adapted to reflect the specific context of factory assembly, ensuring clarity when addressing robot functionalities (Schmidmaier et al., 2024). For instance, adapting "stops when detecting danger" to ensure it reflects the specificity of robot capabilities in a factory setting.

2. **Integration of Interaction Modalities**: Items adjusted to include aspects of visual and gestural feedback based on the robot's functionalities (Putta et al., 2022; Charrier et al., 2019). Phrasing was modified to represent the interaction modes—gesture, voice, and visual displays—where applicable.

3. **Emphasis on Collaborative Dynamics**: Some items were added to focus on turn-taking dynamics, supported by findings related to collaborative tasks where the flow of work is integral (Schmidmaier et al., 2024). 

Each adaptation is traceable to explicit sources within the expert and literature PDFs provided.

## Brief Rationales for Modifications
- **Enhanced Contextual Relevance**: Items were revised to ensure clarity in a factory assembly context, informed by findings from Schmidmaier et al. (2024).
- **Additional Focus on Turn-Taking**: New items included to reflect pacing and collaboration patterns as identified in Putta et al. (2022).
- **Intensified Attention on Hesitation Mirroring**: Adaptations made to ensure reflection of nuanced behavioral responses as per Charrier et al. (2019).

## Notes for Validation
- Suggest conducting pilot testing with a sample of human workers to refine item clarity and relevance.
- Employ confirmatory factor analysis to validate the scale structure.
- Ensure assessment of reliability through Cronbach's alpha and test-retest methodologies.
